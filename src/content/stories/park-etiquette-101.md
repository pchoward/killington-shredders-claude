The park is the most social terrain on the mountain. Everyone's lapping the same features, watching each other, learning from each other. A few unwritten rules keep it fun — and keep people from getting hurt.

## 1. Scope it before you send it

Before you hit any feature, ride past it once. Look at the takeoff, the landing, the speed you'll need and how it's been shaped today. Features change through the day as they get used, warm up, or freeze. The orange oval with **S**, **M** or **L** tells you the size — start small and work up.

## 2. Call your drop

Wait your turn at the top of a feature line. When it's clear, call "dropping!" (a raised hand works too) so the rider behind you knows to wait. Never drop in on someone who's already going.

## 3. Never stop on a landing

Landings and the area right after a feature are the most dangerous places to stand: riders coming off the feature often can't see you until they're in the air. If you fall, get up and clear the landing fast. Sit and chat **off to the side**, uphill of features, where you're visible.

## 4. Easy style it

Do the trick you can land, not the one you saw in an edit last night. Smooth and controlled beats big and sketchy. Progression comes from reps.

## 5. Respect gets respect

Respect the features, the people who build them, the other riders and the signage. Don't ride closed features, don't build your own kicker in the middle of a run, and hype people up when they land something.

---

These ideas line up with the ski industry's **Park Smart** messaging: *make a plan, look before you leap, easy style it, and respect gets respect.* Every resort's park has its own posted rules — read them, and follow them.
