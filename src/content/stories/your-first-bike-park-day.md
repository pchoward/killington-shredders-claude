Lots of park skiers and snowboarders ride bikes all summer, and plenty of the skills carry over: reading terrain, staying loose, landing on the downslope. Here's how to make your first lift-served bike day a good one.

## Rent before you buy

Downhill bikes take a beating, and so do your first-day knees. Rent a full-suspension downhill bike with a **full-face helmet, knee pads and gloves** for your first few days. Goggles help on dusty days. Rental shops will set up the suspension for your weight; let them.

## Start green. Really.

Bike trails are rated just like ski trails: **green circle, blue square, black diamond, double black**. A bike blue is not a ski blue. Start on a green flow trail, do a few laps, then step up. You'll progress faster riding smooth than crashing on something too steep.

## What carries over from the park

- **Stay centered and loose.** Knees and elbows bent, heavy feet, light hands.
- **Look ahead.** Same as lining up a rail: your eyes pick the line, the bike follows.
- **Berms are banked turns.** Commit, lean the bike more than your body, and carry speed out.
- **Jumps:** don't pull on the bars at takeoff. Let the lip do the work, just like a kicker.

## Trail etiquette

- **Ride open trails only.** Closed means closed, for trail crews, events or conditions.
- **Stay off wet trails.** Vermont dirt turns to grease in spring and after rain, and riding it ruins the trail. If it's muddy, ride something else or come back tomorrow.
- **Don't stop in the landing.** Same rule as the snow park: pull over where riders behind can see you.
- **Respect the builders.** Trails are built and maintained by crews and volunteers. If your group runs a dig day, show up.

## Then come ride with us

Summer group rides and dig days get posted in the Killington Shredders Facebook group. All levels welcome, and we regroup at every junction.
